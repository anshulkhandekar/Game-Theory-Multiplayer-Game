# Project design

## Confirmed requirements

The October 13 workshop targets 20–40 laptop players and a final 25–30-minute activity. Everyone participates in host-controlled Crowd Rush → Battlefield → Poker, accumulating individual tournament points. This milestone implements the shared foundation, a separate third-person lobby, all five Crowd Rush rounds. Final tournament normalization is unresolved; `earnings` is raw Crowd Rush money, never labeled tournament points.

Shared: identifiable names, stable identity separate from names, panda/polar/brown/black variants, initial colors/hats, shared 3D lobby, WASD/Space, names rendered safely, help, authenticated host, public projector, readiness, practice/scored rounds, pause/resume, results and lobby return. Server owns movement, timers, phases, occupancy and scores; client requests cannot set position or earnings. Outcomes remain independent from scene effects.

Crowd Rush: three reachable vaults, announced pools, free switching, deadline occupancy, equal splits, zero outside, no awards for empty vaults, no tournament effect from practice, current count/potential payout, explanatory results, configurable rewards and duration. The five-round progression starts flat and adds elevated vaults, route choices, moderate parkour, quick respawns that cost time and longer timers where needed. No player combat. Environmental puncher knockback was added at the user’s request after officer feedback.

Battlefield deferred: preferably three-person teams with adjacent sizes; friend requests plus balanced assignments; each team contests three fronts against three opponents; players own fronts and request/transfer resources; equal total team budgets regardless of size; greater strength wins, ties split; budgets reset per volley, territory accumulates; three understandable units initially; explicitly balance distinct mechanics before unlocking options; visible prep, short private final orders, simultaneous animated resolution; no elimination; equal event score for all teammates.

Poker deferred: standard two-player Kuhn, three-card deck, one private card each, one-chip ante/bet, no raises; contextual check/bet/call/fold; alternate seats and rotate opponents; scored rules remain stable; private cards sent only to their owner; an officer can occupy an odd seat without prize eligibility.

## Provisional implementation decisions

- A single room/process. Node game engine is separate from WebSocket transport and Three.js rendering. Future activities should add their own phase/state handlers when implemented; no generic tournament framework yet.
- 20 Hz server simulation/broadcast, speed capped at 6.8 units/s, acceleration 25 and braking 32 units/s², normalized input, 14-unit lobby/flat arena boundary, gravity 20, jump velocity 7.2. Holding Space does not auto-repeat jumps. Occupancy uses the chamber’s rotated interior footprint (local |x| <=2.39, z between -2.39 and 2.55) with feet within 0.45 of its floor height, in all rounds. A jumping bear may temporarily leave the scoring volume; the server’s deadline sample decides payout.
- Shared server/browser movement rules with local prediction and smooth position corrections; remote interpolation with at most 100ms of velocity extrapolation. Prediction is approximate, without input-sequence replay. LAN response and long-latency behavior need real-device tuning.
- Shared procedural geometry for all bear variants, color scarves, optional caps/crowns, an overhead round-1 camera and third-person lobby/rounds-2–5 camera (parkour host/projector uses an overview), limb gait, smooth facing, jump poses, landing squash, dust particles, walk-in vault chambers and occupancy lighting, lights/shadows and a persisted low-quality toggle. No imported character rig or production animation yet.
- Default 1.5s scene transition when changing arenas, 3s countdown, 20s practice; scored defaults 35/45/50/60/70s for rounds 1–5; pools bronze 120, gold 240, diamond 360. Host may repeat rounds and override duration/pools. Settings reset on server restart.
- Public player UUID distinct from private random reconnect token. Token stored locally and sent only in owner identity reply, excluded from snapshots/projector. Same-token duplicate connection evicts old connection. Names are limited to 24 characters and escaped before DOM rendering; canvas labels are plain text. Messages capped at 4KB, requests rate-limited, input bounds checked, host key checked server-side per socket. Public views have no private game fields; future poker needs owner-specific payloads, not public snapshots.
- Late joins during countdown/play spectate until the next round; reconnecting an eligible player retains location and eligibility. Missing movement expires after 250ms. Disconnects stop input and are excluded from deadline occupancy/payout; reconnect before deadline restores participation. Unresponsive transport is closed by ping/pong heartbeat (up to ~20s detection). Lobby return resets positions/readiness, preserving earnings.
- Finishing changes phase immediately, making scoring idempotent. Atomic JSON replacement persists player identity/appearance/earnings on joins, disconnects and phase/host changes. Server restarts return to lobby and abort unfinished rounds. Single process only; disk failures currently fatal, no transactional database or backup/recovery UI. Host should retain STATE_FILE and back it up between workshops.

## Deferred / known limitations

This is a playable foundation, not final Fall Guys/Roblox polish. Remaining: further hazard/layout variety; further movement/camera tuning from officer feedback; authored rigs and higher-quality animation/audio; LAN/full-laptop stress rehearsal; sequence-based prediction; host roster sorting; room/reset/export tools; transactional durability and observability; HTTPS deployment; Battlefield and poker; normalization and final tournament leaderboard. No player-player collision/congestion physics yet: strategy is about payout congestion, not physical blocking. Crowds can overlap and labels can be crowded. The lobby is a separate timber-and-slate lounge. Furniture, platforms, and chambers use shared solid collision boxes. Posters and trim are visual details; perimeter buildings sit beyond reachable play surfaces. Clearing local browser storage loses automatic identity recovery. Browser profiles share identity across tabs. JSON state grows with every new identity; an archival/reset mechanism is future work. Fonts optionally load from Google Fonts with local fallbacks; game assets otherwise ship locally.

## Five-round Crowd Rush MVP

Confirmed: keep overhead round 1; use third-person lobby and rounds 2–5; camera-relative WASD, independent arrow look, Space jump; farther-apart vaults and increasing parkour difficulty; solid collisions; walk-in chambers with clear entry/exit confirmation; cozy lobby polish, lighting, and game-theory posters. No combat or player elimination. User-requested environmental punchers can launch players; player attacks remain absent.

Provisional visual direction: slate `#344b5b`, steel `#607782`, timber `#a78763`, print paper `#e3e7df`, warm lamps `#ffdda7`; bronze/gold/diamond accents only identify destinations. Existing Barlow HUD stays quiet. Memorable elements are the framed vault chambers, with grates, ribbed walls, wheel fittings, and illuminated threshold/floor. Posters are local canvas textures on physical wall planes (coordination payoff example, reward sharing, and choice/observation). Lobby includes books, mugs, standing lamps and ceiling bulbs. No club branding or slogans.

World definitions live in `shared/world.js`. `src/environment.js` renders those dimensions. Collision uses a 0.32-radius / 2.05-height character box, rotated solid boxes, and <=1/120-second substeps: sides prevent penetration, crossed tops support feet, undersides stop upward jumps. Moving platforms carry grounded riders; spikes reset them rather than award visual-only hits. Geometry samples are bounded-cache shared across the server’s players. Character-to-character collision is intentionally absent, so crowds cannot grief or trap teammates.

| Round | Layout            | Seconds | Platform width | Added challenge                                             |
| ----- | ----------------- | ------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Three chambers    | 35      | Flat floor     | Learn shared reward pools                                   |
| 2     | Platform routes   | 45      | 2.8            | Assisted small ledges, rising routes, one moving stone      |
| 3     | Three-way gallery | 50      | 2.55           | Equilateral vault layout, staggered routes, moving stones   |
| 4     | Punch works       | 60      | 2.35           | Wall punchers with animated wind-up and sideways launch     |
| 5     | High vaults       | 70      | 2.15           | Long routes, wider/faster movers, punchers and floor spikes |

Floor spikes in round 5 cycle every 5s: 2.8s safe, 0.8s fluttering amber wind-up, then a fast strike, hold and retract. Round 4 uses wall punchers; round 5 combines both. Punchers have route-dependent 5/5.37/5.74s periods and staggered offsets. They apply a 9-unit/s sideways launch plus 5.2 upward velocity, with 0.45s momentum protection and a 1.4s contact cooldown. Jump clear or wait for retraction. Visual extension and collision share the authoritative clock; pause freezes movement, carry, hazard animation and cooldowns. Falls below -6 and floor spike hits return players to spawn, costing time but no existing earnings. The camera shortens its boom when visible world meshes obstruct it; it never changes physics outcomes.

Host selects any round for testing or uses Next round from scored Results → Ready → Start. Advancing applies that round’s duration preset, editable before starting. Practice never advances the scored sequence. Arena selection is separate from attempt serial (`round`) and completed scored attempts (`scoredRound`); the visible leaderboard accumulates all scored attempts in this STATE_FILE, including repeats. Restart resets the sequence to lobby/round 1 but preserves identity/appearance/earnings.

Focused tests cover collision sides/undersides, rotated entrance/back-wall/exit behavior, spike warning/hits, all five lifecycle transitions, pause, reconnect, authorization boundaries, exactly-once scoring, and traversal of every route in rounds 2–5 using bounded movement inputs with the actual collisions/hazards enabled. Load tests simulate 40 clients through all five layouts; they do not certify 40 rendered laptops or campus/WAN performance.
